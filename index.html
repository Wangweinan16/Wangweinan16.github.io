01 / Research

A conversation between
electronics and the brain.

Building tools to connect neural activity across space and time—from individual cells to distributed circuits, and from fast dynamics to chronic studies.

01

Small footprints. More connections.

High-density interconnects patterned with electron-beam lithography, designed to expand the bandwidth of ultraflexible neural probes.

02

From one thread to many.

Automated, configurable implantation of multiple nanoelectronic thread probes to reach distributed brain circuits.

03

Interfaces that stay connected.

Integrated systems for chronic neural recording and stimulation, bringing together probes, implantation tools, and ASIC electronics.

01EBL-NETsNanofabrication increases channel density while preserving small, ultraflexible neural probes.Manuscript in preparation+

We use electron-beam lithography to pattern dense interconnects on flexible polymer probes for chronic recording and stimulation.

View project and figures
02CentimaniA precision robot implants flexible neural threads in parallel across distributed targets.Manuscript in preparation+

Centimani brings together independent positioning, optical targeting, and parallel insertion to place flexible electrode arrays across multiple brain regions. Weinan Wang built the robot in collaboration with Yu Wu, Romain Albouy, and Curtis Chen.

View project and figures
03GChipA custom ASIC integrates high-channel-count recording and programmable stimulation with an ultraflexible probe array.SfN 2026 poster+

Four ultraflexible shanks connect to a custom ASIC that records neural activity and delivers programmable current pulses. Bench measurements characterize the hardware, and two in-vivo recordings show how the system resolves single units and measures their responses to stimulation. Gerald Topalli of Taiyun Chi's lab developed the GChip ASIC.

View project and figures
